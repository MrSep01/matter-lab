For the same substance, changing solid → liquid → gas requires energy. During a change of state at constant pressure, potential energy changes while temperature and average kinetic energy stay constant.

FIRST: WHAT DOES TEMPERATURE MEASURE?

Temperature tells us about average motion.

Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. A thermometer measures temperature, usually in degrees Celsius (°C).

Kinetic energy = energy of movement

Particles move, so they have kinetic energy. For particles of the same mass, faster movement means more kinetic energy. Solid particles have kinetic energy too: they vibrate.

Average = the mean

Particles in one sample do not all have the same kinetic energy. Add their kinetic energies, then divide by the number of particles.

Average kinetic energy = total kinetic energy ÷ number of particles

On a sloping heating curve: temperature rises because average kinetic energy increases. Particles move faster on average.

On a phase-change plateau: temperature and average kinetic energy stay constant. Energy changes the arrangement of particles and their potential energy.

Remember: hotter does not mean every particle moves faster than every particle in a cooler sample.

Does a bigger sample mean a higher temperature?

No. Four particles with total kinetic energy 16 units have an average of 4 units. Eight particles with total kinetic energy 32 units also have an average of 4 units. They have the same temperature in this model: temperature depends on the average, not the total.

Why is there no temperature change during a phase change?

On a heating plateau

Energy overcomes intermolecular attractions: forces that pull molecules together. Potential energy increases. Average kinetic energy does not increase. The temperature stays constant (the same).

On a cooling plateau

Molecules come closer together or become more ordered. Attractions form. Potential energy decreases. Energy leaves the substance. The temperature stays constant until the change finishes.

The water molecules stay H₂O. Melting and boiling do not break the bonds inside each water molecule.

Both make a gas. Different processes.

Water vapour is invisible. The visible mist above hot water consists of tiny liquid droplets.

EVAPORATION

An escape from the surface

Some molecules at the surface have enough kinetic energy to escape. This can happen below the boiling point.

Higher-energy molecules escape. The liquid left behind has lower average kinetic energy, so it can cool.
BOILING

Vapour forms throughout

At the boiling point, vapour bubbles form throughout the liquid. The bubbles rise to the surface.

Keep heating: more liquid becomes gas. At constant pressure, the temperature stays the same until all the liquid has boiled.
